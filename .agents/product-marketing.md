## Target Audience
**Target companies:**
- **SaaS:** seed to Series B startups, usually 5–100 people, without an in-house video team
- **Law firms:** small to mid-size firms and solo practitioners (personal injury, family, immigration, estate and employment law are the most active video marketers) **[CONFIRM practice areas]**
- **Financial advisors:** independent advisors, small RIAs/wealth teams, insurance and planning practices
- Geography: Toronto / GTA first; remote across Canada and the US. **[CONFIRM]**

**Decision-makers:** SaaS: founder/CEO, Head of Marketing, Product Marketing Manager. Law: managing partner, marketing coordinator. Advisors: the advisor/principal themselves.
**Primary use case:** Turning something complicated (a product, a legal process, a financial plan) into a short, polished video that builds trust and drives booked calls/demos.
**Jobs to be done:**
- "Explain what we do in 90 seconds so prospects get it and book a call"
- "Make us look as credible and premium as we actually are"
- "Give me a steady stream of video content without me learning to edit"
**Use cases:**
- SaaS: homepage explainer, product demo/walkthrough, feature launch video, onboarding/tutorial videos, customer testimonial videos, social cut-downs, motion graphics / UI animation
- Lawyers: firm and attorney intro videos, practice-area explainers, FAQ series ("What happens after a car accident?"), client testimonials (where permitted), LinkedIn/YouTube content
- Financial advisors: "meet your advisor" video, client-education series, market/quarterly update videos, seminar and webinar edits, social clips

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** These businesses sell complex, high-trust services, and text and stock photos don't build trust. Video does, but most of their video looks cheap or never gets made.
**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Freelancers on Fiverr/Upwork: cheap, but uneven quality and no strategy
- Big video agencies: great work, but $15k–$50k+ and slow **[CONFIRM price range for your market]**
- DIY (Loom, phone, Canva/Descript): fast, but looks amateur, which erodes trust for lawyers and advisors in particular
- Template explainer-animation shops: generic and interchangeable
**What it costs them:** Lower website conversion, prospects who "don't get it", fewer booked calls, and looking less credible than competitors.
**Emotional tension:** Discomfort on camera, fear of looking unprofessional, compliance worries (lawyers/advisors), and "we tried video before and it flopped."

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** B2B/SaaS video agencies and legal/financial marketing agencies that bundle video. They fall short because they're expensive and slow, or because video is an add-on done by generalists.
**Secondary:** Freelance editors and animators. They fall short on consistency, polish and project management.
**Indirect:** DIY tools (Loom, Descript, Canva, AI video/avatar tools such as Synthesia and HeyGen). These fall short because output looks generic, and AI avatars can undermine trust in high-trust industries.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- **Hollywood VFX background:** 20 years compositing and finishing feature films. That level of polish is rare at a small-business budget.
- **Photographer's eye:** strong lighting, framing and colour
- **Teacher:** college instructor in Toronto, and good at making complex things simple (the core skill of an explainer video)
- **Direct access to the senior artist:** no account-manager layer
**How we do it differently:** Senior craft plus clear storytelling, at small-agency speed and price.
**Why that's better:** Videos that look premium make the business look premium, and in trust-based industries that directly affects who books the call.
