## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** These businesses sell complex, high-trust services, and text and stock photos don't build trust. Video does, but most of their video looks cheap or never gets made.
**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Freelancers on Fiverr/Upwork: cheap, but uneven quality and no strategy
- Big video agencies: great work, but $15k–$50k+ and slow **[CONFIRM price range for your market]**
- DIY (Loom, phone, Canva/Descript): fast, but looks amateur, which erodes trust for lawyers and advisors in particular
- Template explainer-animation shops: generic and interchangeable
**What it costs them:** Lower website conversion, prospects who "don't get it", fewer booked calls, and looking less credible than competitors.
**Emotional tension:** Discomfort on camera, fear of looking unprofessional, compliance worries (lawyers/advisors), and "we tried video before and it flopped."

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** B2B/SaaS video agencies and legal/financial marketing agencies that bundle video. They fall short because they're expensive and slow, or because video is an add-on done by generalists.
**Secondary:** Freelance editors and animators. They fall short on consistency, polish and project management.
**Indirect:** DIY tools (Loom, Descript, Canva). These fall short because the output looks amateur.
**Self-serve AI avatar tools** (Synthesia, HeyGen): these fall short because the avatars look generic or uncanny and the client still has to do all the scripting, editing and finishing. Pixels Speak builds the avatar from coached, well-shot footage and finishes it to film standards.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** Homemade-looking videos, a freelancer who disappeared, agency quotes that were too high.
**Pull:** Film-level polish, a senior person doing the work, 72–120 hour turnaround, and an avatar so they don't have to film every week.
**Habit:** "We'll just record a Loom", "maybe next quarter."
**Anxiety:** Wasted budget, awkwardness on camera, compliance risk, a new vendor with no portfolio in their industry, and worry that an AI avatar looks fake or could be misused (consent, likeness rights).
